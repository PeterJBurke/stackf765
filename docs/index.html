StackF765

An open-hardware ArduPilot flight controller: STM32F765, two stacked 31.5 × 48 mm boards on a 20 × 20 mm mount, plus a small USB / buzzer / boot board on a cable. Every pin is on a labelled 0.1 inch right-angle header.

At a glance

ProcessorSTM32F765VIT6, 216 MHz Cortex-M7, 2 MB flash
FirmwareArduPilot (Copter / Plane / Rover), board name StackF765
SensorsICM-45686 IMU (SPI), BMP388 barometer (I2C)
Battery2S–3S LiPo (6–13 V), 50 A continuous through a 0.5 mΩ shunt
Serial ports7 UARTs + USB, all on labelled header pins
Outputs6 motor / servo outputs (DShot capable) on standard 3-pin G / 5V / SIG columns, addressable LED
VideoAnalog OSD (AT7456E): camera in, VTX out
LoggingmicroSD (4-bit SDMMC)
5 V rails3 A for the flight controller and peripherals; a separate 3 A for a companion computer
Size31.5 × 48 × about 19 mm; 20 × 20 mm grommet mounting
Service boardUSB-C, buzzer and DFU button on a 6-wire cable
Parts costabout $55 per set at single-unit prices (PCBs and assembly extra)

Pinout

Labels are printed on the boards. 5V pins are live only on battery; 4V5 pins are also live on USB, which is handy for setting up a GPS or receiver on the bench.

Default ArduPilot mapping: SERIAL0 = USB, SERIAL1…7 = UART 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8. Any port can be GPS, telemetry, receiver or ESC telemetry; set SERIALn_PROTOCOL. Full table: docs/pinout.md.

Build one

  1. Order the three PCBs with assembly using the files in fab/. Settings and stack-ups: docs/manufacturing.md.
  2. Solder the two right-angle headers and the battery / ESC wires, then stack the boards: docs/assembly.md.
  3. Power up on a current-limited supply and work through the checks in docs/bringup.md.
  4. Flash the bootloader and ArduPilot: docs/firmware.md.

How the circuits work, with the calculations: docs/design.md.
